1. About DigitalOcean: The Developer's Cloud Partner

Founded in 2011 by Ben and Moisey Uretsky, DigitalOcean is an American cloud infrastructure provider that revolutionized the industry by focusing relentlessly on developer simplicity. Headquartered in New York, DigitalOcean went viral in 2013 by being one of the first cloud companies to offer pure SSD-based virtual machines. Today, it empowers millions of developers, startups, and SMBs worldwide by providing scalable computing power, storage, and networking without the overwhelming complexity associated with legacy cloud providers like AWS. Known for its comprehensive tutorials, transparent pricing, and predictable billing, DigitalOcean makes it incredibly simple to deploy and scale applications rapidly.

2. DigitalOcean’s Best-Selling Products

DigitalOcean’s core product is the Droplet, which are highly scalable, Linux-based virtual machines that provide root access and can be deployed in under a minute. Their basic Droplets are incredibly popular, starting at just $4 per month (512 MiB RAM, 1 vCPU, 10 GiB SSD). For containerized applications, their Managed Kubernetes (DOKS) is a best-seller, offering a free control plane and worker nodes starting at $12/mo. Additionally, the App Platform is highly favored by developers wanting a Platform-as-a-Service (PaaS) experience; it offers a free tier for up to 3 static sites and dynamic apps starting at $5/mo.

3. DigitalOcean vs Raff Technologies: Pricing & Features

When selecting scalable cloud infrastructure, developers frequently compare DigitalOcean to agile competitors like Raff Technologies. While DigitalOcean sets the industry standard for community support and ease of use, analyzing pure computing costs reveals distinct differences. DigitalOcean utilizes metered transfer limits (e.g., a $24/mo droplet includes 4TB bandwidth with $0.01/GB overage fees). In contrast, competitors like Raff offer unmetered bandwidth on similar tiers. Furthermore, DigitalOcean charges an extra 20% of the Droplet's monthly cost for automated backups, whereas some smaller competitors bundle backups for free.
